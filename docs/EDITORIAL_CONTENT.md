# Editorial astronomy content

The private catalogue keeps the published editorial copy in the French
`description` field and in localized `content.en.description` and
`content.ja.description` fields. It remains distinct from
`technicalDescription`, which is reserved for acquisition, processing and
instrument information.

Each published object description has two concise paragraphs:

1. what the image shows and the relevant astrophysical process;
2. a rounded distance or physical scale when it is established, plus a useful
   historical catalogue, observation or dynamical milestone.

For galaxies, add the relationship that makes the object scientifically
distinct when it is well established: its group or companion, a tidal or
bar-driven process, an unusual motion, or its role in the Local Group. For
example, M31 is the Milky Way's nearest major galaxy and its blueshift records
its approach. Do not imply that it is the nearest galaxy of any kind: several
dwarf galaxies are closer.

Distances and physical sizes are deliberately stated as orders of magnitude.
They vary with the adopted measurement, and diffuse nebulae do not have one
universal physical boundary. Do not turn an approximate public-facing value
into a precision claim.

## Research sources

Check and refresh object facts against primary or curated astronomical sources:

- [SIMBAD at CDS](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/), for identifiers, object
  type, literature-backed measurements and cross-identifications;
- [NASA’s Hubble Messier catalogue](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/explore-the-night-sky/hubble-messier-catalog/), for accessible
  historical and physical context on Messier objects;
- original catalogue references and their scholarly records for the NGC, IC,
  Lynds and Messier history.

When a new fact is added, write it naturally and independently in French,
English and Japanese. Preserve object identifiers, measurements and original
acquisition data exactly.
